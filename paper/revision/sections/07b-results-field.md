### 7.3 Where the administrative trace lives

Table 4's first column presents focal isolation as unbiased. That rests on a claim with a known limit, stated in Section 4.3: back-dating of the focal event can reach $r^{\star}$ through a neighbouring group's record, which no reading of the focal register opens. The objection is load-bearing, so we made the accession wave a controlled parameter and swept it against six widths, four homophily settings and five seeds in each of two convention regimes: 840 corpora.

Three truths are graded against. The *focal-record horizon* is what a per-group audit reads. The *every-register horizon* is what the pipeline computes, since focal isolation runs over every group's record. The *administrative horizon* is the truth: the largest lead time at which any record carries a dyad from the acceding entity into the focal group's members that the honest registers do not, **counting only dyads the focal event caused**. That restriction is not a convenience, because any group's back-dating puts administrative dyads into $r^{\star}$ at arbitrary lead times, and calling those a leakage horizon would be the error this paper is about. Table 5 reports the sweep.

| one | 0 | 0.00, max 0 | 0.00, max 0 | 0 percent | 0.0 percent |
| one | 0.1 | 0.00, max 0 | 0.00, max 0 | 0 percent | 0.0 percent |
| one | 0.25 | 0.00, max 0 | 0.00, max 0 | 0 percent | 0.0 percent |

**Table 5.** Where the administrative trace lives, by dating convention and wave share. 840 corpora over six widths, four homophily settings and five seeds.

Under one dating convention the claim holds exactly. In all 480 corpora of that regime, **not one event** carried an off-focal trace the focal record did not already carry. The reason is simple once measured: the partner register is back-dated by the same width as the focal one, so every dyad the wave puts into $r^{\star}$ early is one the focal record already shows.

Under mixed conventions the claim fails, by up to nine periods. Give the partner register the trade register's coded gap distribution against a sharp focal width, and the focal-record reading undershoots the administrative horizon by a mean of 0.82 periods at a wave share of a tenth, 2.22 at a quarter and 2.68 at a half. This is corpus A's situation, not a contrivance: that corpus dates 7,615 accessions exactly and 447 only to a five-year block, inside one target relation. The failure is administrative and not a form of homophily. Under mixed conventions the mean gap is 3.77, 2.07, 2.67 and 2.23 periods at homophily 0, 1, 2 and 3; under one convention it is 0.00 at all four. A reference band that removed homophily perfectly would leave this untouched.

What closes most of the gap is procedural. Figure 4 puts the two readings against the truth in both regimes. Graded against the administrative horizon, the every-register reading errs by $-0.81$, $-0.87$ and $-1.32$ periods at the three wave shares, so it now exceeds the truth by about a period instead of falling short by two or three. That is the direction the asymmetric loss prefers, and the remedy is cheap because focal isolation reads a membership register and no graph.

The pooled statistics are not a substitute, and this is the result we would have wanted to go the other way. They read $r^{\star}$ and therefore do see the off-focal dyads, yet against the administrative horizon the clique estimate undershoots in 39 to 53 of 120 cells and the residual rule in 53 to 91 of 120. Seeing a dyad is not the same as attributing it. Two caveats remain. The experiment cannot say which regime corpus A is in as a magnitude, and the examined range of eight periods censors focal isolation itself here, because the partner law reaches nine.

### 7.4 A width printed in the source

The trade register of Section 6.3 codes the back-dating width per agreement, and asking the same question two ways shows the two instruments parting company on it.

Measured on the focal agreement alone, presence is 1 for every lead time inside the coded gap and 0 outside it, at every gap width. That is entailed by the construction, since the relation is dated from signature, the event from entry into force, and the gap is their difference. It verifies the pipeline without recovering the width independently, and Table 1 records it as such. The entailment is what makes the comparison sharp: the width is in the data by construction, so an instrument that misses it is missing something that is definitely there.

Measured the way the suite specifies, as a density over the whole relation, the contrast between inside and outside the coded gap is **0.1023**, with a 95 percent interval of 0.0905 to 0.1139 over 758 agreements. The curve is nearly flat, running 0.7519 at zero lead time and falling only to 0.6206 at eight periods. Agreements with a coded gap of three periods still show 91 percent presence eight periods out. Figure 5 puts the two measurements side by side.

The mechanism is a share, and the share is the result. The back-dated dyads are 6.8 percent of the relation, so a density over the whole relation is a weighted average in which the quantity of interest has weight 0.068. That is why the pooled curve is flat where the focal curve is a step, and it is the whole explanation. We state it as a share and not as a ratio, because the obvious ratio does not survive a look at its units: the focal reading is a presence probability and the pooled reading a dimensionless density contrast, so their quotient of 9.77 measures how far apart two instruments read, not any fraction of a width. The same comparison on corpus A gives 0.9844 against 0.1447, a ratio of 6.80. What both corpora support is weaker and more useful: the group's own record and the pooled relation disagree by close to an order of magnitude on the same events.

### 7.5 The coalition corpus: a phantom horizon at scale

We ran the suite as specified on corpus A, then ran focal isolation on the same events. What this corpus cannot do is worth stating first. It cannot falsify the pooled reading by comparison with focal isolation, because the register codes one date per membership for 94 percent of its events, and a statistic silent by construction cannot contradict one that speaks. Nor does the obvious repair exist: the one stratum in which focal isolation measures consists entirely of organisations founded before 1965, so the deepest lead time at which the pooled statistic could be read on those events inside the window is zero. This is the selection fact of Section 2.3. Three of the four results below need no focal isolation at all.

The first is that the target relation is indistinguishable at eight periods from a relation that cannot record the event. Table 6 reports the matched contrast by relation and lead time on 52,305 positives, with negatives drawn from the same group and period. The statistic is the difference in the mean share of the candidate's dyads into the existing members that the relation carries, positives minus matched negatives, at level 1. Figure 7 plots the full curves.

Trade flows cannot record an accession to an intergovernmental organisation. They nonetheless produce a monotone decaying contrast retaining 55.4 percent of its value at eight periods, as does voting agreement at 60.8 percent. At eight periods the target relation's interval is 0.1160 to 0.1768 and trade flows' is 0.1161 to 0.1755. At zero lead time they are separated, 0.2504 to 0.3773 against 0.2415 to 0.2825, so the two series do part company where the event is; they have simply stopped doing so by the far end of the examined range. What every non-flat series measures is that entities acceding to a group are more similar to its members than never-joiners are, and were so a decade earlier.

The one flat relation is the second result. Militarised disputes sit at 0.004 at every lead time with an interval two thousandths wide, and the interval at eight periods overlaps the interval at zero. This is the cleanest evidence that the instrument is not simply blind: the one relation that can record neither the event nor the similarity is the one relation the diagnostic separates from the target, and it is the relation a documentary reading would have nominated in advance.

The third is that the crossing lead time is not identified. The residual first falls below the threshold at $\Delta = 5$, so the pre-specified rule returns about five periods. The resampling does not support that five. Across 1,000 family-level replicates the crossing lead time has a median of 4 and a 95 percent interval spanning 0 to 7; it never crossed inside eight periods in 222 replicates, and in 133 the residual was already below the threshold at zero lead time, meaning there was no phantom at all. **The phantom is real in most samples and its width is not identified.**

The fourth is that the unconditioned curve's four-period decay is recurrence. Pooled over all accessions, the declared-resolution stratum decays 0.3490, 0.2489, 0.1175, 0.0448 — the textbook shape of a four-period horizon with a floor. It is prior membership spells. What is specific to a leakage diagnostic is that the repetition manufactures a curve with the shape of a horizon instead of merely inflating a score, and that it loads onto whichever stratum recurs most. That stratum's recurrence rate is 30.65 percent against the exactly dated stratum's 6.16 percent, so the artefact loads onto precisely the stratum that appears to have a horizon, which is also the stratum whose dates are least precise. Figure 6 shows the same events before and after conditioning.

Against all of this, focal isolation carries no trace beyond one period. Conditioned on first-time entry, the trace is present at zero lead time and exactly absent at every lead time of one or more, over the 7,146 exactly dated accessions. For 94 percent of the corpus that zero is an entailment and not a measurement. The exception is the declared-resolution stratum, which reads **0.1419** at one period and 0.000 beyond, over 310 events in 42 families, with a 95 percent interval of 0.0678 to 0.2395. The exactly dated stratum reads 0.0 at the same lead time with an interval of zero width, which is what an entailment looks like beside a measurement. The register declares its own pre-1965 dates accurate only within five-year blocks, and the coding rule that locates an event inside such a block is exactly the curatorial decision this paper is about. **The width is small, it is one period, and it is real. A diagnostic reporting eight would be wrong by a factor of eight.**

Saturation is why this was inevitable here. Of the 474,034 candidate-to-member dyads the pooled statistic reads as present at $k = 1$, **117** are present only through the focal entity — 0.02 percent. At the declared primary level $k = 25$ the figure is 6.5 percent. The level therefore decides the answer by a factor of 260, and at the level a reader would assume, the focal group has contributed one dyad in four thousand of what the statistic reads.
