## 10. Limitations

**No comparison against another leakage diagnostic.** We report the suite against ground truth and against itself rather than against a competing instrument, because we could not find one. We are aware of no published statistic that takes a relation and a lead time and returns how far a register's dating reaches. The four estimators graded against each other in Table 4 are therefore variants of our own rule, including the one a practitioner who skipped Section 4.2 would compute. Its absence is a limitation of this paper and not a claim about the literature.

**The second domain is a scope test and not a replication.** Corpus B tests the size and homophily mechanisms and cannot test anything about lead time, so no central measurement has been reproduced on a second corpus. The generator supplies a lead-time axis with a known truth and partly closes the gap, but it is synthetic. It can show that homophily destroys identification, and Section 7.2 answers on a scale both sides have whether any real corpus reaches that strength. What it cannot do is show that corpus A's own phantom is *caused* by homophily, because the trace there is the unknown.

**The central field corpus cannot falsify the pooled reading, for a structural reason.** Corpus A codes one date for 94 percent of its events, so focal isolation is silent there by construction, and the one stratum in which it does measure lies entirely before the analysis window. The falsification against a known width therefore rests on the trade register alone, with 759 agreements in a domain that is not the paper's primary one. A register coding two dates over a corpus of corpus A's size would settle what neither of ours can, and we know of none.

**The anticipation channel is not measured.** Focal isolation measures back-dating introduced by what a source records as a date, and only where a source codes two dates. Anticipation through other channels, such as an entity establishing bilateral ties before joining, remains unmeasured, and no contrast of the form we study separates it from homophily.

**The magnitudes have intervals; the design decisions do not.** Every headline magnitude carries a percentile interval from a bootstrap over the declared resampling unit, and one of those intervals is wide enough to matter. What carries no uncertainty statement is the set of choices upstream of the statistics: the anchor set, the level ladder, the comparison strata and the window. Those were fixed in writing beforehand, which prevents them from being tuned to the result without making them right. A different documentary reading of the same five relations would produce a different band, and we have no way to put an interval on that.

**The declaration is timestamped for one corpus and not for the other.** Corpus A's pre-registration is in version control with dates preceding the data. Corpus B's channel declaration is not, so Section 7.6's prospective reading is a claim about our own procedure that a reader cannot check, and we have downgraded what we claim from it accordingly.

**One implementation, cross-checked against a second.** One implementation computed the clique contrast and group density across all four testbeds, which makes cross-domain comparison interpretable and also meant an implementation error would have been common to all results. We have since written a second implementation from the definitions, sharing nothing with the first: it stores dyads in dictionaries where the original packs them into sorted integer arrays, and it enumerates every pair of every candidate group where the original decomposes the group into a cached member-only part and a per-candidate star. The two agree exactly on every value in every testbed, and the calibration of Table 3 was re-derived end to end through the second. What the cross-check found was not an arithmetic error but the specification gap Section 5 now closes, and it narrows the exposure without removing it, since both implementations were written by the same people from the same prose. No one outside the author group has run the pipeline or rebuilt the corpus.
