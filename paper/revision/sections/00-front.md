# Leakage horizons in group membership prediction: why pooled density diagnostics cannot measure them, and what can

## Abstract

Forecasters of group membership — which state joins an organisation, which protein joins a complex — draw their relational features from the registers that also supply their labels. Because registers are compiled retrospectively and date their contents by conventions of their own, a membership dated from the signing of a founding convention rather than from the year an organisation began work places an edge in the feature graph before the event that edge encodes. A correct temporal split does not remove this, since the contamination lies inside the register and not across the split. We call the smallest lead time at which the target relation carries no administrative trace of the event the **leakage horizon**, and ask whether it can be measured.

The instrument implied by the leakage literature is a pooled one: compare relational density between positives and negatives at increasing lead times, and read off the point at which the difference dies. We show that it does not measure the horizon. On a generator in which the true width is set and homophily is a controlled parameter, the pooled statistic's least-squares slope on that width falls from 1.00 to 0.00 as homophily concentrates, because a trace of the event and a standing similarity between the candidate and the members enter the statistic identically. On a trade-agreement register coding both a signature and an entry-into-force date, so that the width is observed rather than assumed, the back-dated dyads make up 6.8 percent of the relation and the pooled reading is dominated by the rest. On a coalition corpus of 562 groups it reports contamination after eight years, where the group's own membership record carries none past one. A statistic read on that record stays unbiased in homophily throughout, subject to two conditions we measure rather than assume, and we set out five procedures for the common case of a register that codes a single date.

**Keywords:** label leakage; temporal validation; group membership prediction; graph neural networks; measurement validity; valid time; data provenance
