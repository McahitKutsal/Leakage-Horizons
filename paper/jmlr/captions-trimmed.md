## Table 3

- Column five is a \textbf{coverage rate} and should be read as one: it is the share of resamples in which the rule lands exactly on the target, so its complement is the share in which the reported horizon is wider than the true one. *(defines 'coverage rate'; the body names it too)*
- Every miss is upward, which is why the sixth column's intervals have the target as their lower endpoint.
- The last block replaces the sharp width with one drawn per event from the trade register's own coded gaps, where ``the width'' is a choice and each candidate definition is graded separately.

## Table 4

- Each cell aggregates six widths and reads mean error / slope / censored, where \textbf{censored} counts the widths at which the estimate sits at the top of the examined range of eight periods. *(defines 'censored'; the body names it too)*
- The column is here because a slope computed over cells that are all censored is mechanically zero, and a table showing only the slope cannot distinguish an output that does not depend on the truth from a range too narrow to hold the output.
- Every 1.00 in this table is uncensored, so those cells are the strong reading; the collapsed cells are fully censored, and Table 5 settles them by widening the range.
- The residual rule is the rule Section 6.5 applies to corpus A; the unbanded rule is the same statistic with no anchor band, which is what a practitioner who skipped Section 3.2 would compute.
- Each cell is one corpus at one seed and carries no sampling interval, unlike Tables 3, 7 and 8.
- What stands in for one is Table 5, which reruns the whole grid at three seeds and its transition rows at five, and the shape of the table itself: a monotone collapse reproduced independently in both anchor regimes.

## Table 8

- Negative cliques are given as integer counts rather than as rates, because a rate of 0.0000 cannot distinguish ``none observed'' from ``none possible''.
- The expected-count row is \(\sum_{i}^{}d^{\binom{n_{i}}{2}}\) over the \textbf{realised} sizes \(n_{i}\) of the negatives in the bin, at the channel's own density \(d = 0.001153\), rather than at the bin's lower edge: the exponent grows quadratically in size, so the edge would understate the row by orders of magnitude in the larger bins. *(defines 'realised'; the body names it too)*
- The basis is stated so the row is checkable.
- The statistic has power where groups are small and is structurally zero where they are large.
