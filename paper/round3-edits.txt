Group membership forecasters draw relational features from the registers that produced their labels, so a register dating an event early encodes the label before it exists. A correct temporal split does not remove this: the contamination sits inside the register, not across it. We formalise its reach as a \textbf{leakage horizon}, the smallest lead time at which the target relation carries no administrative trace of the event, and build diagnostics to estimate it. Against a width a generator sets, and therefore knows, the estimate lands on it in all six settings and never short of it: a conservative estimate, not the lower bound we specified, whose upper end is not identified. The suite also misreports. On a coalition register the pooled reading still shows a contrast after eight years, where the group's own record carries nothing past one year. Ten ways such a diagnostic misreports follow, one undemonstrated and four tested on a protein complex benchmark.
